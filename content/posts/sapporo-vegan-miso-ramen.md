I visited Sapporo a few years ago and had some of the best ramen of my life. What makes Sapporo ramen distinctive is its rich miso-based broth, the wok assembly method that chars the tare before mixing with soup, thick curly noodles that can stand up to the heavy broth, and toppings like sweet corn and butter — a nod to Hokkaido's dairy and agricultural heritage. The wok method is particularly important: instead of mixing tare and soup in the bowl, you char the miso tare against a hot wok surface, creating Maillard-driven caramelized flavors you simply can't achieve any other way.

This recipe is heavily inspired by the component-based approach in "The Book of Ramen" by Michael T. Satinover, adapted for a fully vegan bowl. The core philosophy remains: precise measurements, component separation, and understanding why each technique matters.

## Sources

> The Book of Ramen; Michael T. Satinover and Scott J. Satinover

## Ramen Bowl

Makes 1 bowl

- 300 milliliters Vegetable Chintan, hot
- 60 milliliters unsweetened soy milk
- 80 grams vegan miso tare
- 30 milliliters ginger and onion oil
- 140 grams thick curly ramen noodles, cooked
- 10 grams vegan butter
- Toppings: sweet corn, blanched bean sprouts, sliced scallions, nori, menma

### Sapporo Miso Wok Method

The wok method is the defining assembly technique of Sapporo miso ramen. Have everything ready at your station before you start — the vegetables cook rapidly and timing is critical.

1. Preheat your serving bowl (oven at 77°C/170°F, or fill with boiling water and drain).
2. Start cooking the noodles in rapidly boiling water.
3. While the noodles cook, heat a wok over high heat. Add the ginger and onion oil.
4. Add a handful of bean sprouts and any other vegetables you'd like to sauté. Stir-fry for 20-30 seconds.
5. Add the miso tare to the wok. Let it hit the hot surface and char briefly, 15-20 seconds, until fragrant and just darkening at the edges. This charring creates toasted, caramelized compounds that define Sapporo ramen's flavor.
6. Deglaze with the hot Vegetable Chintan. Stir vigorously to incorporate the tare.
7. Add the soy milk and stir. Do not let it return to a full boil.
8. Drain the noodles thoroughly — shake the basket hard to remove excess water.
9. Place the noodles in the preheated bowl.
10. Pour the soup and tare mixture from the wok over the noodles.
11. Pile the sautéed vegetables on top. Add corn, scallions, nori, menma, and a pat of vegan butter.

## Vegetable Chintan

A light, clear vegetable stock built on kombu, dried shiitake, and aromatics. The stock is deliberately light in body — the miso tare and soy milk add the richness and viscosity.

- 3 liters water
- 1 medium onion, peeled and halved
- 1 carrot, peeled, cut into large chunks
- 10 cloves garlic, peeled
- 1 5-centimeter piece fresh ginger
- 10 grams dried shiitake mushrooms
- 1 bunch green onions
- 20 grams kombu

1. Add the water, onion, carrot, garlic, ginger, and dried shiitake to a pot.
2. Bring to a boil over high heat, then reduce to a simmer.
3. Cook at a simmer (around 95°C) for one hour.
4. Add the green onions and kombu, turn off the heat, and let steep for 15 minutes.
5. Strain the stock and reserve until needed.

Yield: approximately 2.5 liters (enough for about 7 bowls at 350 mL per bowl plus soy milk).

**Umami Without Animal Products**: Kombu provides glutamate while dried shiitake provides guanylate (GMP). When combined, these create a multiplicative umami amplification — the same synergy principle used in traditional dashi, but with plant-based ingredients. This glutamate-guanylate synergy is the foundation of shojin ryori (Buddhist temple cuisine).

## Vegan Miso Tare

A three-miso blend with caramelized onion, raw garlic and ginger, soy sauce, mirin, and doubanjiang. The caramelized onion is critical — it converts sharp raw onion into a deeply sweet, complex base that rounds out the miso.

- 140 grams raw onion, pureed in a food processor (approximately half an onion)
- 7 grams garlic, grated (approximately 2 cloves)
- 6 grams ginger, grated (approximately a 2-centimeter piece)
- 300 grams white miso (shiro/shinshu style)
- 180 grams mugi miso or tezukuri miso
- 80 grams hatcho miso (or dark red miso)
- 20 grams mirin
- 30 grams soy sauce
- 2 grams doubanjiang (chili bean paste)
- 1 gram black pepper (approximately 20 grinds from a mill)
- pinch of white pepper
- pinch of togarashi or cayenne

1. Take 70 grams of the raw onion puree and add to a small pan.
2. Cook over low heat, stirring frequently, until golden brown, around 20-30 minutes. This caramelization step develops deep sweetness and complexity.
3. Remove from heat, place the cooked onion in a mixing bowl.
4. When the onion has cooled, add all remaining ingredients — the raw onion puree, garlic, ginger, misos, mirin, soy sauce, doubanjiang, and peppers. Use a whisk to combine.
5. Place in the fridge in a covered container and allow the tare to mature for at least 24 hours. This rest period removes harshness from the raw vegetables and allows the flavors to integrate. The tare will keep for up to 12 months.

Use 80 grams of tare per bowl.

**Three-Miso Blend**: White miso (shinshu) provides sweetness and body, mugi/tezukuri adds rustic grain-forward character, and hatcho contributes deep, dark fermented intensity. If you can't find all three, a 60/40 blend of white and red miso is a reasonable approximation, but the full three-miso blend is noticeably more complex.

## Ginger and Onion Oil

A simple aromatic oil infused with ginger, garlic, and onion. Traditional Sapporo miso ramen uses rendered pork lard, which acts as both flavoring and an insulator trapping heat in the bowl. Vegetable oil performs the insulating function but lacks lard's distinctive richness — the vegan butter melting on top of the finished bowl helps compensate.

- 125 milliliters neutral vegetable oil (such as grapeseed or canola)
- 1/2 medium white onion, cut into large pieces
- 4 cloves garlic, peeled
- 1 3-centimeter piece fresh ginger, peeled, sliced into thin coins

1. In a small saucepan, add the oil, onion, garlic, and ginger.
2. Heat over medium to medium-low heat, stirring occasionally, until the ingredients take on a light golden hue and smell fragrant, around 15 minutes.
3. Remove from heat and allow to cool.
4. Strain and transfer to a container. Keep in the fridge for up to 6 months.

Use 30 milliliters of aroma oil per bowl.

## Toppings

### Sweet Corn

- 80 grams sweet corn kernels (fresh, frozen, or canned)

If using frozen, thaw and drain. If fresh, blanch briefly or char in a dry skillet for added sweetness. Hokkaido is Japan's primary corn-growing region, and corn became inseparable from Sapporo's signature dish.

### Blanched Bean Sprouts

- 100 grams mung bean sprouts

Blanch in boiling water for 30 seconds, drain immediately. Reserve a portion to sauté in the wok during assembly — these get charred flavor from the wok method.

### Other Toppings

- Scallions, sliced into thin ringlets or fine dice
- Nori
- Menma (bamboo shoots)
- Vegan butter (10 grams per bowl, placed on top at serving)
- Optional: chili oil for extra heat
